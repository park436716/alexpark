## Summary

Judge Sidney H. Stein (S.D.N.Y., presiding over MDL 25-md-3143 *In re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*) denied OpenAI's Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss the Consolidated Class Action Complaint's output-based copyright infringement claim. The court held that plaintiffs — a class of book authors including George R.R. Martin, John Grisham, Jonathan Franzen, Michael Connelly, and Jodi Picoult — plausibly alleged that ChatGPT's plot summaries and derivative outlines are substantially similar to the copyrightable elements (plot, setting, characters) of their novels.

## Key Contributions

- **First appellate-track ruling** treating LLM outputs (not merely training-set ingestion) as potentially infringing derivative works surviving a motion to dismiss.
- Adopts the **"more discerning observer" test** for LLM outputs mixing protectable and non-protectable content.
- Establishes that a **complaint's allegations plus outputs submitted with opposition briefing** are enough at the pleading stage — outputs need not be attached to the complaint if they are "clear, definite, and substantial\[ly\] reference\[d\]" and "integral" (Chambers v. TimeWarner; Lateral Recovery, 632 F. Supp. 3d 402).
- Distinguishes *N.Y. Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp.*, 777 F. Supp. 3d 283 (S.D.N.Y. 2025) (news summaries capturing only non-protectable facts).
- **Fair use is expressly reserved** — to be decided on a developed factual record.

## Methodology and Architecture

**Prima facie claim (Yurman Design)**:
1. Actual copying (satisfied by CCAC ¶¶ 97–99 access/training allegations).
2. Substantial similarity between defendant's work and *protectable* elements.

**Substantial similarity framework (Peter F. Gaito; Boisson; Abdin)**:
- Compare "total concept and overall feel" — theme, characters, plot, sequence, pace, setting (Williams v. Crichton).
- "More discerning observer" filters non-protectable stock characters, scènes à faire, abstract ideas (Walker v. Time Life Films).
- Works themselves supersede pleading characterizations (Peter F. Gaito, 602 F.3d at 64).

**Rule 12(g)(2) preliminary issue**: Successive Rule 12(b)(6) motions permitted where re-filing under Rule 12(c) would be inefficient (Ballast v. Workforce7).

## Results

- **Motion to dismiss DENIED** as to output-based infringement claims.
- Court reproduces a full ChatGPT summary of *A Game of Thrones* — a "detailed summary" that "parrot\[s\] the plot, characters, and themes" — and holds a more discerning observer could easily conclude substantial similarity.
- Court reproduces a 15-point ChatGPT outline for a fabricated sequel *"A Dance with Shadows"* to *A Clash of Kings* — "no doubt" a reasonable jury could find substantial similarity based on setting, plot, and characters carried over from Martin's works.
- Court expressly notes that even the "ordinary observer" test would yield the same result (fn. 2).
- Only "some" outputs suffice to defeat the motion — many other outputs plaintiffs alleged are "significantly less detailed."

## Doctrinal Significance

- Provides an early roadmap for pleading LLM output-infringement claims at the 12(b)(6) stage.
- Signals that **detailed plot summarization and derivative outlining** — as distinct from short factual summaries — cross the threshold of substantial similarity for pleading purposes.
- Distinguishes *NYT v. Microsoft* on style/tone/facts vs. plot/setting/character grounds, narrowing that earlier grant of dismissal.
- Leaves **fair use** — the industry-defining question — for summary judgment.
