## One-line Summary

Judge Stein (S.D.N.Y., MDL 25-md-3143) denied OpenAI's Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss authors' output-based copyright infringement claims, holding that plaintiffs plausibly alleged that certain ChatGPT-generated summaries and derivative outlines are substantially similar to the protectable elements of the authors' books.

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **Output-based infringement can survive Rule 12(b)(6)** even without attaching the offending outputs to the complaint, if the outputs are (a) incorporated by "clear, definite, and substantial reference" and (b) integral to the complaint's allegations.
2. **"More discerning observer" test applies** because plaintiffs' works contain both protectable (character, plot, setting) and non-protectable elements (stock themes, scènes à faire).
3. **Detailed plot summaries and derivative outlines can be substantially similar to novels**: The court reproduces a ChatGPT summary of *A Game of Thrones* and an outline for a fabricated sequel *"A Dance with Shadows"* to *A Clash of Kings*, and holds a jury could find substantial similarity.
4. **Distinguishes N.Y. Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp., 777 F. Supp. 3d 283 (S.D.N.Y. 2025)**: There, news-article summaries only captured non-protectable facts and differed in style, tone, length, sentence structure. Here, outputs incorporate protectable plot, setting, and characters.
5. **Rule 12(g)(2) does not bar successive Rule 12(b)(6) motions** where re-filing under Rule 12(c) would be inefficient (Ballast v. Workforce7 Inc.).
6. **Fair use not decided**: Opinion is expressly agnostic; fact-sensitive inquiry deferred to summary judgment (citing Graham v. Prince; Harper & Row; TCA Television).

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

Standard Rule 12(b)(6) plausibility analysis (Iqbal, Twombly) plus Second Circuit copyright doctrine:

- **Prima facie infringement (Yurman Design)**: (1) actual copying + (2) substantial similarity between defendant's work and *protectable* elements of plaintiff's.
- **Actual copying** satisfied by allegations of access + training on plaintiffs' works (CCAC ¶¶ 97-99).
- **Substantial similarity** analyzed under "more discerning observer" (Boisson v. Banian; Abdin v. CBS), comparing "total concept and overall feel" (Peter F. Gaito), not merely dissected copyrightable atoms.
- **Documents considered on 12(b)(6)**: Complaint plus incorporated-by-reference documents plus documents integral to the pleadings (Chambers v. TimeWarner; Rowe Plastic Surgery).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

- ChatGPT-generated summary of Martin's *A Game of Thrones* (~1.5 pages reproduced in the opinion): court holds a more discerning observer could conclude substantial similarity based on setting, plot, characters, and "overall tone and feel."
- Alternative-sequel outline *"A Dance with Shadows"* (~15 bullet points): court holds no doubt a reasonable jury could find substantial similarity.
- Even under the "ordinary observer" test, the same conclusion (footnote 2).
- Plaintiffs pleaded similar allegations for 15 named authors: Baldacci, Branch, Connelly, Day, Franzen, Golden, Greer, Grisham, Hwang, Klam, Martin, Picoult, Schiff, Shapiro, Eberhart/Authors Guild.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- Opinion is on a 12(b)(6) motion — accepts plaintiffs' allegations as true; does not decide the ultimate merits.
- **Fair use expressly not decided**; will be adjudicated on a developed factual record.
- Whether the *quality* of summarization matters (many outputs are described as "significantly less detailed") remains open; the court needed only "some" outputs to survive.
- Damages, class certification, and injunctive scope are still pending in the MDL.

## 6. Related Work

- N.Y. Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp., 777 F. Supp. 3d 283 (S.D.N.Y. 2025) — distinguished (news-article summaries not substantially similar).
- Twin Peaks Prods. v. Publ'ns Int'l, 996 F.2d 1366 (2d Cir. 1993) — detailed plot summary can infringe.
- Penguin Random House LLC v. Colting, 270 F. Supp. 3d 736 (S.D.N.Y. 2017) — same.
- Peter F. Gaito Architecture v. Simone Dev., 602 F.3d 57 (2d Cir. 2010) — "total concept and feel" standard.
- Boisson v. Banian, 273 F.3d 262 (2d Cir. 2001) — "more discerning observer" test.
- Authors Guild v. OpenAI (23-cv-8292) — underlying case, consolidated into this MDL.

## 7. Glossary

- **CCAC** — Consolidated Class Action Complaint (Dkt. 183).
- **MDL** — Multidistrict Litigation.
- **Rule 12(b)(6)** — Federal Rule of Civil Procedure permitting dismissal for failure to state a claim.
- **Rule 12(g)(2) / 12(h)(2)** — Rules governing successive motions and preserved defenses.
- **Substantial similarity** — Legal standard for the second element of copyright infringement.
- **Scènes à faire** — Scenes flowing necessarily from a chosen setting or situation; not copyrightable.
- **More discerning observer test** — Applied where the work mixes protectable and non-protectable elements.
- **Prima facie claim** — Allegations sufficient on their face to state a cause of action.
